# Campaign plan: LedgerFlow AI waitlist launch

LedgerFlow AI is a made-up practice brand. This plan is for practice only. We never send email to real people.

## 1. Campaign overview

- **Campaign name:** Your Team Stays in Control
- **Summary:** Over four weeks of email, we invite our waitlist to start an Accounting Workflow Automation subscription.
- **Main goal:** 300 waitlist members start a subscription by Sunday 3 January 2027.
- **Other goals:**
  - Learn which of the three groups replies and subscribes most, so we know who to focus on next.
  - Collect the questions people ask, so we can answer them on the website and in later emails.
- **Dates:** Monday 7 December 2026 to Sunday 3 January 2027 (4 weeks).
- **Budget:** Small. Email only. No paid ads.

**A note on the goal:** 300 out of about 2,000 people is 15% of the list. We have no past results to compare it with, so this is a target and not a forecast. We check progress every week (see section 7) and change the plan if we fall behind.

## 2. Target audience

About 2,000 people who joined the LedgerFlow AI waitlist. They already know our name, so this is a **decision-stage** campaign: they don't need to hear about us, they need a clear reason to start now.

We split them into three groups. Suggested group labels for the leads file are in brackets.

| Group | Who they are | What slows them down | What they want |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small business owners (`small-business-owners`) | Owners who do their own books or have one bookkeeper. They use QuickBooks or Excel. | Hours spent matching bank statements by hand. They worry about mistakes and don't have time to learn new tools. | Less repetitive work and books they can trust, without losing control. |
| Accounting firms (`accounting-firms`) | Small and mid-sized firms that keep books for many clients. | The same reconciliation steps repeated for every client. Busy periods at month end and year end. | Faster reconciliation with review, and an audit trail they can show clients. |
| Finance managers (`finance-managers`) | People who run the finance team inside a business. | Month-end close takes too long. Spreadsheets break and errors are hard to trace. | Exception reports, traceable records, and a team that stays in charge of approvals. |

We don't yet know how many people are in each group. Count them in the leads file before week 1 starts.

**Where they spend time:** their email inbox, QuickBooks and Excel, and talking with their accountant or finance team. Email is the right channel because they asked to hear from us.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** We automate repetitive accounting tasks, verify the results, and keep your finance team in control of every important decision.

**Supporting messages:**

1. **Automate repetitive work, not financial judgment.** We handle the matching and checking. Your team makes the decisions.
2. **Every transaction can be traced back to its source.** Each result links to the bank line or record it came from, with an audit trail.
3. **You see what needs a human.** Anything that doesn't match goes into an exception report for your team to review.
4. **Open about our limits.** We say which QuickBooks and Excel setups we have tested, and what we don't do.

**Proof points:** We only use results we have checked. Until we have checked results we are allowed to share, we show *how* the work is checked (the steps, the exception report, the audit trail) instead of quoting numbers. We don't name clients or use testimonials without written permission.

**Message by group:**

| Group | Opening angle |
|---|---|
| Small business owners | "Spend less time matching bank statements, and still check every result yourself." |
| Accounting firms | "Run the same reconciliation steps across your clients, with an audit trail for each one." |
| Finance managers | "Get exception reports before month-end close, so your team reviews only what needs attention." |

## 4. Channel strategy

| Channel | Why it fits | Format | Effort | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Email to the waitlist | These people asked to hear from us. It costs very little and leads straight to a sign-up. | Short plain-text-style emails with one main link each. | Low to medium | Almost all of it: the sending tool and our writing time. |
| Subscription page on our website | Every email links here. People decide here. | One page: what's included, how review works, what we've tested, questions and answers. | Medium | Writing time only. |
| Replies to emails | B2B buyers often ask questions before they decide. A quick, honest answer helps. | Personal replies from the team. | Medium | Team time. |

**Not used:** paid ads, influencers and events, because the budget is small and the brief says email only.

No money goes to paid ads.

## 9. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | How we handle it |
|---|---|
| **The holiday weeks are quiet.** Weeks 3 and 4 fall over Christmas and New Year, when many people are away. | Put the main messages in weeks 1 and 2. Keep the week 3 and 4 emails short. Don't send on public holidays. |
| **Accounting firms are busy at year end.** They may not have time to decide in December. | Make their week 2 email short and practical. Invite them to reply with questions so they can decide when they're ready. |
| **We fall short of 300.** 15% of the list is a high target with no past results to back it. | Check the weekly numbers. If we're behind after week 2, move effort to the group that responds best, and talk to the people who replied. |
| **We say more than we can prove.** Launch emails can easily overpromise. | Run every email through a brand review against brand/voice.md before sending. Describe only features that are ready, and only setups we've tested. |
| **Questions about privacy or tax rules.** Finance buyers will ask. | Answer privacy questions from the documented safeguards only. Send tax and regulation questions to a qualified professional. |
